    Usage

    Input format

    ParShift reads conversations from CSV files (with read_ccsv()) or from Pandas data frames with one utterance (message) per row and the following columns:

    Column Description
    utterance_id ID of the utterance (integer)
    speaker_id ID of the speaker
    utterance The utterance itself (text)
    reply_to_id or target_id See reply and target modes below

    An empty value (or None) in the reply_to_id or target_id column means the utterance is addressed to the group. Additional keyword arguments of read_ccsv() are passed to Pandas read_csv(), e.g., sep=";" for files separated by semicolons.

    Reply and target modes

    The addressee of each utterance is given by one of two columns, which define how ParShift interprets the conversation:

    • reply_to_id (reply mode): ID of the utterance being replied to. The addressee is the speaker of that utterance. This is the format of chats and forums where messages reply to other messages.
    • target_id (target mode): ID of the speaker being addressed. This is the format of annotated conversations where the addressee is known.

    If both columns exist, reply_to_id is used and target_id is ignored.

    For example, the same conversation in both modes:

    Reply mode:

    utterance_id,speaker_id,utterance,reply_to_id
0,10,Hello everyone!,
1,11,Hi!,0
2,10,How are you?,1

    Target mode:

    utterance_id,speaker_id,utterance,target_id
0,10,Hello everyone!,
1,11,Hi!,10
2,10,How are you?,11

    Both give the participation shift codes A0-XA (speaker 11 answers a remark to the group by 10) and AB-BA (10 answers back).

    Turns

    Participation shifts are computed between turns, not between utterances. A turn is a sequence of consecutive utterances by the same speaker to the same addressee (or to the group). In reply mode, the addressee is the speaker of the replied utterance, so consecutive replies to different utterances of the same speaker are in the same turn.

    The first turn of a conversation has no participation shift code, since there is no previous turn. In reply mode, a turn is compared with the turn it replies to; otherwise, it is compared with the previous turn.

    annotate() only assigns the 13 participation shifts defined by Gibson. For this purpose, the following turns are considered as addressed to the group, and a warning with their number is issued:

    • turns addressed to their own speaker, e.g., a speaker replying to their own utterance, or targeting themselves;
    • in reply mode, turns replying to an utterance which is not in a previous turn, e.g., an utterance outside the data, in truncated or segmented conversations.

    Known limitations

    • IDs in reply_to_id and target_id must be integers. Other values raise a ValueError. In target mode this means that speakers must be identified by integers, so that they can be addressed.
    • In target mode, speaker IDs must be written as the integers in target_id. Speaker IDs are compared as text, without leading and trailing spaces, while targets are converted to integers. For example, a speaker 007 is not recognized as the target 7.
    • Speaker IDs are otherwise opaque: they can contain any text, including spaces and commas. Leading and trailing spaces do not distinguish speakers.
    • Parshift.process() splits a conversation in at most 4 parts (N between 1 and 4), with approximately the same number of turns each.
